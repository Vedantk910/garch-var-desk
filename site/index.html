How I built it.

The arc from a volatility question to a desk a client can re-run.

  1. Frame one risk question."Is the 99% one-day tail defensible, and where does it break?" reduced to two estimators (historical vs GARCH) on two statistics (VaR and Expected Shortfall), backtested on a shared out-of-sample window.
  2. Source only reproducible numbers.SPY daily log returns from 2015-01, winsorized at the 0.1%/99.9% quantiles, cached offline; the GFC 2008-09 and 2020-Q1 stress windows on the same SPY calendar. Every figure ships in out/desk_numbers.json, regenerated by one command, no network.
  3. Model with the right distribution first.GARCH(1,1) with a Student-t conditional beats the Gaussian on AIC (7334 vs 7540) and flags near-integrated persistence (0.994). A normal GARCH mis-prices the tails, so its 99% VaR/ES is too tight - the desk shows that, it doesn't hide it.
  4. Prove it, not just claim it.The Kupiec POF, the Christoffersen independence test and the Mendez-Gaona joint likelihood-ratio all run on the desk's own 552-day window; a no-look-ahead test pins the iterated stress forecast invariant to future data. 35 offline tests, seeded, one-command re-run.
  5. Answer the hostile question.The deflated Sharpe corrects the raw 0.68/0.56 for the three arms tried on the panel: after the haircut neither clears 5%. The stress re-fit names where the model breaks - GFC persistence already at the 1.0 boundary, ES over-shooting the shock - instead of hiding it. That admission is the elite move.
  6. Make it feelable.python run_desk.py regenerates the numbers; this page re-implements the same closed-forms in JS, re-simulates the overlay from the pinned panel, and a Node harness pins the two languages to the same bits.

Three attacks, answered with a number.

What I'd get asked in a finance interview - and the defence that holds.

Attack 1

"A 7-of-552 breach rate is just luck on a 99% level - prove the coverage isn't a fluke."

The Kupiec POF runs the exact null (expected 5.52 hits) and gets z = 0.63, p = 0.53 - neither over- nor under-conservative. The Christoffersen independence test confirms the hits don't cluster. And the Mendez-Gaona joint test on the VaR/ES pair checks the middle loss band too, which a VaR-only POF can't see - chi-square 1.94 on 2 df, p = 0.38. Three tests, one conclusion: the level is honest.

Attack 2

"Your Sharpe is 0.68 - but you tried three models and three overlays, so it's data-mined."

Correct - that's exactly what the deflated Sharpe prices. It corrects the null for every arm tried on the panel (M = 3): the expected best-of-three under H0 is non-zero, so a raw Sharpe above zero is not automatically signal. After the correction the 15% vol-target p-value is 0.17 and risk-parity's is 0.34 - neither clears 5%. The edge is real, not proven. That is the honest read, and the number is re-runnable for any M.

Attack 3

"Your 2008/2020 stress window is only 44-54 days - you can't validate a 99% level on that."

And I don't claim to. A 99% POF over 44 days expects ~0.44 hits, so a non-rejection is not validation - the power note says so in the output. The stress re-fit's point is different: the calm model is already at the persistence boundary (1.0000), so a crash has no persistence left to raise, and the ES level over-shoots the realised tail loss for weeks. The desk reports the model's admission of its limits, not a false pass.
